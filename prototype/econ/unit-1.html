Opportunity cost

Wants exceed resources, so every choice has a trade-off.

The opportunity cost of a choice is the value of the next-best alternative you give up, plus any extra money you pay for that choice. Skip costs you would pay either way.

Accounting profit \(=\text{revenue}-\text{explicit costs}\). Economic profit also subtracts implicit costs, such as a wage you give up.

Check yourself

You skip a $20 shift to see a $12 movie. Dinner would have cost $10 either way. What is the opportunity cost of the movie?

On, inside, or outside

A point on the frontier is efficient. A point inside is feasible but inefficient. A point outside is unattainable with current resources and technology.

A linear PPF has constant opportunity cost: \(Y=Y_{\max}-\dfrac{Y_{\max}}{X_{\max}}X\). The cost of one more \(X\) is \(\dfrac{Y_{\max}}{X_{\max}}\) units of \(Y\).

A bowed-out PPF has increasing opportunity cost: each extra pizza costs more robots than the last.

Comparative advantage

Comparative advantage

Absolute advantage is the ability to produce more with the same resources. Comparative advantage is the ability to produce at a lower opportunity cost.

The opportunity cost of one unit of \(X\) is \(\dfrac{\text{output of }Y\text{ per hour}}{\text{output of }X\text{ per hour}}\) units of \(Y\).

Mutually beneficial terms of trade for \(X\), measured in units of \(Y\) per \(X\), lie strictly between the two opportunity costs. Specialize, then trade, and compare with autarky (no trade).

Check yourself

Ava’s opportunity cost of one bread is 0.5 cloth. Ben’s is 2 cloth. Who has the comparative advantage in bread?

Equilibrium, shortages, and surplus

Price is on the vertical axis; quantity is on the horizontal axis. For \(Q_d=A-BP\) and \(Q_s=C+DP\),

\[P^*=\frac{A-C}{B+D},\qquad Q^*=A-BP^*.\]

If the posted price is below \(P^*\), \(Q_d>Q_s\): a shortage. If it is above \(P^*\), there is a surplus.

For inverse demand \(P=a-bQ\) and supply \(P=c+dQ\), consumer surplus is \(\tfrac12(a-P^*)Q^*\) and producer surplus is \(\tfrac12(P^*-c)Q^*\).
