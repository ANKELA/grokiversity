Forces as vectors

You push a box with a rope

The rope is not level. Drag the end of the rope. Watch how much of your push goes along the floor, and how much lifts.

A force is a push or a pull. It has a size and a direction.

Tap the box, the rope, or the floor to name them.

Along the floor
Lifting up

A steeper rope lifts more, and pulls the box along the floor less.

Size and direction, as one arrow

We draw a force as an arrow. The length is the size. The way it points is the direction.

The usual unit is the newton (N). A small apple weighs about one newton.

Angle up from the floor

Drag the arrow tip. There are no formulas yet — just get a feel for size and direction.

The same push, split in two

Any angled push can be replaced by two simpler pushes: one along the floor, and one straight up. Together they do the same job.

Each of those simpler pushes is called a component — a part of the force in one direction.

Straight up

Turn a part off and on. The original arrow does not change.

A right triangle hides in the arrow

Look at the triangle made by the arrow and its two parts. The long side is the original push. Drag the tip, then reveal the math one piece at a time.

We will start with a concrete number: a 100 N push.

Check yourself

Use the idea you just built. A 100 N push acts at 30° above the floor. How big is the along-the-floor part?

Hint: it is 100 times the cosine of 30°. Cosine of 30° is √3 / 2 ≈ 0.866.

The lifting part uses sine

The side standing up is opposite the angle. So the lifting part is the size times the sine of the angle.

For the same 100 N push at 30°: the lift is 100 × sin 30° = 100 × 0.5 = 50 N.

In symbols, after that example: \(F_y = F\sin\theta\). \(F_y\) is just a name for “the up-and-down part.”

Now you try. Same 100 N push at 30°. How big is the lifting part?

A lamp hangs on two strings

The lamp is not moving. Drag it left, right, or down. Watch the strings. The tighter string is the one that has to pull harder.

If something is not starting to move, the pushes and pulls on it cancel. We say it is in equilibrium.

Drag the lamp. The strings get tighter as they flatten.

Not moving means the forces balance

If 20 N pulls down, the upward parts of the other forces must add to 20 N. Sideways parts must cancel too.

We write that as \(\sum F_x=0\) and \(\sum F_y=0\). The capital sigma means “add them all up.” The 0 means “they cancel.”

A 5 kg lamp hangs still. What is its weight? Use \(g=9.81\ \mathrm{m/s}^2\), and \(W=mg\).

A force can also twist

Drag the box along the seesaw. The farther it sits from the middle, the more that side wants to go down.

That twisting effect is a moment. A moment is bigger when the force is bigger, and when you apply it farther from the turning point.

Distance from the pivot
Moment (twist)

The pivot is the triangle in the middle. Try both sides.

A wrench: moment is force times distance

When you push straight across the handle, the moment about the bolt is the force times the distance from the bolt.

First a number: 10 N, at 0.50 m, pushing straight, gives \(10\times 0.50=5\) N·m. The unit is the newton-metre (N·m).

Then the general rule: \(M=F\,d\), for a perpendicular push.

Moment \(M=F\,d\)

You can also drag the force arrow and the handle end on the picture.

If you don't push straight

Only the part of the force that is perpendicular to the handle twists the bolt. The part along the handle just shoves into the bolt.

Example: 10 N at 0.50 m, at 30° to the handle. The twisting part is 10 × sin 30° = 5 N, so \(M=5\times 0.50=2.5\) N·m.

In general: \(M=F\,d\sin\theta\), where \(\theta\) is the angle between the force and the handle.

Forces balance, and twists balance

For a rigid body in a plane, three independent statements must hold:

The left-right forces cancel: \(\sum F_x=0\).

The up-down forces cancel: \(\sum F_y=0\).

The twists about a chosen point cancel: \(\sum M=0\).

A rigid body is an object that does not change shape for the purpose of this course — a beam, a wrench, a frame.

A good trick: take moments about a point where an unknown force acts. That unknown drops out of the moment equation.
